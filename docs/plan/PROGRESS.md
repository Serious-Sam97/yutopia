## Map structure upgrade (2026-10-10)
Maps are now **per-space data** (`yutopia_spaces.layout`, null = the built-in Studio Building), edited in build mode. One set of rules in `packages/shared/src/map-edit.ts` + `grid.ts` serves the client preview and the server.
1. **Walls:** Walls tool paints walls, any floor finish, or empty space by click-drag. Refused (with a reason): walling over furniture, people, doors or the arrival point, or removing the wall a board/screen hangs on.
2. **Rooms & desks for a person:** Rooms tool — drag a rectangle → name, owner (project members), sound (private/open/booth), floor, optional walls + door built around it. A sign over the door shows "ROOM · Owner". Desks tool — click any seat to give it to someone: they always land there; a nameplate shows on it (one assigned desk per person).
3. **Doors:** placed in a one-tile gap between walls; open / closed (swings open as people pass) / locked. Only the room's owner locks/unlocks (project owners can unlock in an emergency); **while locked only the owner can walk in or out** — enforced server-side per person (each person has their own walk grid), and pathfinding, teleport and "join them" respect it.
4. **Map size:** presets S 32×24 · M 48×36 · L 64×48 · XL 80×60 · XXL 96×72 or custom 16–96; resize keeps content top-left (furniture beyond the edge removed), or start blank; set the arrival point.
- Yondra: migration `2026_10_12_000001_add_layout_to_yutopia_spaces`, signed `PUT /internal/yutopia/spaces/{id}/layout` (shape-validated), `GET /api/yutopia/spaces/{id}/members`; seeder adds the **Yutopia Lab** sandbox project.
- Tests: shared +5 (painting guards, resize, blank, rooms + locked doors), world-server +3 (paint permissions, owned room + lock incl. a tampered client pushing into the doorway, desks + resize), backend +1, e2e `map-edit.spec.ts` (blank map → room with owner → walls → desk → lock → Bia kept out → resize).

- **G0 — spike.** Emulator: Nostalgist 0.22.0 (MIT, no runtime deps, pinned below the newest release) driving libretro cores from `retroarch-emscripten-build` v1.22.2 — FCEUmm, Snes9x, Genesis Plus GX, Gambatte — plus the 2600's Stella 2014 from EmulatorJS (its loader renamed so Nostalgist takes it; its own input/state calls used). All self-hosted: `tools/emu-assets.mjs` fetches each file pinned by sha256 into `public/emu` (not in git), writes `SOURCES.md` (GPL sources). Every core boots in ≈1 s; both pads drive both kinds of core; save states round-trip. Our own test cartridges (`tools/test-rom-*.mjs`, hand-assembled 6502): colour bars / a backdrop that goes red for pad 1, green for pad 2 (and a 440 Hz tone on the 8-bit one). Main-thread work per frame on the host (rAF callbacks): world 1.4 ms → hosting a 16-bit game 2.0 ms (median; p95 2.2–2.9). The TV stream: ~90 kbps far away, ~110 kbps watched big (retro pictures are cheap); nothing while nobody watches (dynacast).
- **G1 — the look** (image-first: the real art on `/dev/items`): CRT TV on a walnut stand (woodgrain or silver cabinet, knob panel, rabbit ears, a VCR blinking 12:00), woodgrain 2600-style console, 8-bit front-loader and red-and-cream top-loader, grey 16-bit with the colour dots, black 16-bit with the round top, the handheld in its TV dock, the cartridge shelf; cartridges show in the slot in their label colour. Pads, cables, the LED and the picture are live.
- **G2 — plugging in.** Catalog tab **Game room**; consoles cable to a TV within 4 tiles (two inputs, the TV's *Input* switches), unplugged when moved too far; cartridges only fit their console; the ready-made **Game room** (build → My setups → Ready-made: TV, shelf, two consoles, bean bags, sofa, rug, lamp).
- **G3 — play, everyone sees it.** Turning on makes you the host: the ROM comes down (built-in from `/emu/games`, uploads from Yondra), the core runs in your browser, the TV goes out as its own LiveKit participant (`tv-<console>`, token minted by the world-server): picture for the whole space, sound for whoever's in earshot (the world's hearing rules). Every TV in the world shows its console's picture (CRT scanlines and glass, "NO SIGNAL" blue while paused, its glow on the floor at night); the camera leans in over the TV with the picture big in a CRT frame and the controls under it.
- **G4 — multiplayer.** Pad 2 (where the console has one): keys / gamepad / touch → a 12-bit mask through the world-server to the host's emulator; players shown seated with pads in hand, cords to the console. The host leaving (or their tab closing) pauses it; anyone at it carries on as host from the last save state.
- **G5 — library + saves.** Yondra: `yutopia_games` (uploads per space: console, size and extension checked, 128 MB per space, duplicates refused, only who brought it or the owner takes it away), `yutopia_game_saves` (state + battery save per space and game); 17 built-in homebrew games with author, licence and source shown (`shared/library.ts`, `tools/emu-games.json`) + our two test cards. Autosave every 45 s and on power-off; "Turn on where it was left" loads the last state in a new session.
- **G6 — polish.** Phone: the touch pad under the picture; key hints per console; wake-lock noise contained; the TV's video copied into a canvas each frame (Pixi's video textures don't refresh on the engine's own loop).
- **Checked:** shared 44 (new: cartridge rules, cable gap, the glass's turn, library integrity, the ready-made room fits) · world-server 36 (new: plug / cartridge / power / pad 2 relay / audience / carry on) · Yondra YutopiaTest 18 (new: uploads, saves) · e2e `games.spec.ts` 3 (two people: plug in → on → Bia sees the TV's picture *and* receives its sound → pad 2 turns it green on both screens → Ana leaves → Bia carries on; bring your cartridge → play → save → new session → turn on where it was left (the state is loaded); phone touch pad) · all 19 built-in cartridges boot with a picture · typecheck, lint (the remaining `wardrobe.ts` findings predate this) · budget: `yutopia-games` 13.2 KB; the world page 529.8 KB (≈ +2 KB for the hooks; it was already over its 520 KB budget).
- **Deviations:** the 16-bit consoles are two items (each its own core); the 2600 core comes from EmulatorJS (no Stella in the RetroArch build); viewers draw the TV through a copied canvas, not a Pixi video texture; turning on offers "where it was left" (not only after a host leaves); a host whose connection drops pauses the game at once (their emulator went with their tab).
